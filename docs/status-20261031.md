# Where the work stands — 2026-10-31

## 2026-11-01, the round after — what broke, and what was found underneath

Eight things were reported. One of them was a blocker, and finding out why it
blocked turned up a bug nobody had noticed because nothing ever checks it.

### The posts that would not go through

Reproduced from the outside first, with a real account on the live project:
a text post, a photo post and a video post all succeed — through
`POST /rest/v1/rpc/create_post` with a member's own token, through the media
Worker (`/m`, including a 15 MB upload), and through the app itself, driven in a
browser, signed in, composer → Share post → a row in `posts`. So the posting path
is not broken, and the generic sentence on the toast was hiding something else.

What *was* broken, on every request the app makes, was this:

```
GET /rest/v1/crushes?select=id&from_id=eq.33&deep=eq.true&created_at=gt.…
→ 42703  column crushes.id does not exist
```

`getMe()` runs seven reads in a `Promise.all`, and this was one of them: `crushes`
is keyed on the pair `(from_id, to_id)` and has no `id`. The whole of `getMe()`
therefore rejected, `useMe()` never produced a member, and every screen that
reads `me` — the Space, the composer's defaults, the profile rings, the Keep
Close state — ran on `undefined`. It is one column name. It is fixed
(`select('from_id', { count: 'exact', head: true })`).

The upload path also got the things it was missing: one retry on a transport or
5xx failure (a phone on a train is the normal case for an upload), a size limit
with a sentence that says the number rather than a 413, and the raw failure
written to the console so a bug report carries a reason instead of the generic
line.

### The + that was in the wrong place

It was a floating button at `bottom: 100, right: 24` — over the feed, under the
thumb. It is now the middle of the tab bar, between the second and third tabs,
raised and ringed in the page colour. The Space has its own `+` beside Settings,
because that is where people post from.

### The Space that was a card, not a Space

Someone else's Space led with a full 1.22:1 photo with the name printed on it,
then a Keep Close *card* the height of a paragraph, and only then the tabs.
Now: a cover (0.98:1, capped at 460) as the background, a round face on it
carrying the Moments ring, the name and a Keep Close **chip** beside it, then
the tabs — all on the first screen. The card, its two-line explanation and the
standalone ring avatar are gone; the ring is on the face, which is where
everybody already looks for it.

### Text posts in the viewer

A post with no photograph opened a viewer with an empty gradient and the words
stranded at the bottom — which is what a broken image looks like. Words-only
posts now render their words in the middle of the stage. This is also the first
half of the answer to "shared a text post and tapping it opened the post viewer":
it still opens the viewer, but the viewer now has something in it.

### Still to do, deliberately

The composer-as-boards (type first, then a board per type), real tagging and
hashtags, the reel-style vertical viewer, and the global type scale are a round
of their own — each one is a rewrite of a screen, not an adjustment to it.

`tsc` clean; **203 tests pass**.

## 2026-11-01, later — the video frame, and the match moment

Two of the open defects, taken together.

### 1. A landscape clip on the post viewer

The band of black above and below a 16:9 clip was not a rendering bug, it was a
decision written down in a comment: "the stage is the box here … a 16:9 clip on a
phone screen is a band with black above and below, which is correct." It was
correct arithmetic and wrong product — on a screen that is one post and nothing
else, black bands read as a fault.

Now the stage chooses a mode, and the mode decides both what happens to the
picture and what the leftover space is made of (`lib/videoFrame.ts`):

| Mode | The picture | The space around it |
|---|---|---|
| `fit` (default) | whole frame, nothing cropped | a blurred, oversized copy of the same clip |
| `fill` | cropped to the stage | none — there is none left |

`fit` is the default because silently cutting a third off each side of what
somebody filmed is a worse lie than a soft band. The button in the top bar swaps
modes and says which one it will give you: **Fill the screen** / **See the whole
frame**.

Two things had to be fixed to make it draw. `VideoEditCanvas` painted an opaque
black background, so a backdrop given to it was invisible — it now takes a
`backdrop` and goes transparent when it is handed one. And the backdrop cannot be
the same player: two `VideoView`s on one player is not something expo-video
supports on the web, so it is a second muted player, deliberately outside the
focus system because it has no sound to claim.

Measured, not assumed: `leavesBands()` compares the stage ratio with the picture
ratio and a portrait clip on a portrait phone only asks for a backdrop when there
is really a band (evidence: `evidence/video-stage-portrait.png`).

### 2. The match moment

`crush_member` has always returned `{mutual, isNew}` and `connect_pair` has
always opened the Whisper for both people. What only ever existed for one of them
was the *moment*: the person who pressed the button second got
`MutualCrushScreen`, and the person who was crushed on first got a toast that
looked like every other toast — or, if the app was closed, a row in a list they
might not open for days.

Now both halves get the screen (`lib/matchMoment.ts`):

* **Live.** A `mutual` notification opens `MutualCrush` instead of toasting.
* **On launch.** A match that landed while the app was closed opens once, the
  first time the app comes back — unread, under 48 hours old, nobody already
  celebrated this session. It is marked read as it opens, so it cannot greet you
  twice.
* **From the list.** Tapping an unseen match in the notification centre opens the
  moment; tapping one you have already been shown goes to the Space instead.

The badge payload carries a name but no photo, so the screen now asks for the
Space with `useUser` and fills the face in rather than drawing an initial
(`evidence/match-moment-nophoto.png` is exactly that path).

The decision rules are a pure function with eleven tests, because "shown twice",
"replayed on every launch" and "a day-old crush treated as news" are the three
ways this goes wrong quietly.

`tsc` clean; **203 tests pass**, up from 192.

### Four in one push: sharing into a whisper, a Space with tabs, editing a post, and the dots

**1. A post can now be sent into a Whisper.** *Share* used to mean one thing:
hand the words to the system share sheet. But the person you want to show a post
to is usually somebody you are already talking to here. Share is now a choice —
the Whisper you want, or somewhere outside Crushly — and what lands in the thread
is a small square card: the picture, whose post it is, the first line of it, and
"Open post". From the sender's side it is their colour; from the other side it is
theirs. Tapping it opens the post.

The card is built **on the server**, in `send_message` with a new `post` kind
(`20261101000000_share_post_to_whisper.sql`): it looks the post up, refuses
unless `can_view_post` says the sender may see it, and snapshots the author's
name and media into the message. A private post cannot be passed around by
knowing its id, and a recipient always sees what was actually posted rather than
what the sender's phone claimed.

**2. A Space has tabs, and the ring opens the Moments.** Another person's Space
was one long page of facts about a person with nothing they had made on it. It
now has **Posts · Vibes · Moments**, in that order, with counts:

- **Posts** — their posts, in the feed's own card. This is `space_posts()`, an
  RPC that has existed in the database since October and that **nothing on the
  client had ever called**.
- **Vibes** — the clips they still have running, as tiles with a play mark
  (`space_vibes()`, likewise never called).
- **Moments** — the same pebble grid the Moments tab uses, so the two screens
  agree about what a Moment looks like.

The way into their Moments is the **colour ring around their face**, next to
their name — the shape everybody already knows. It used to be a speech bubble,
which said "message them" and sat where the name is.

**3. Posts can be edited.** Until now the only thing you could do to your own
post was delete it, so a typo cost you the post. `edit_post()`
(`20261101000001_edit_post.sql`) rewrites the words and who can see it, and
re-checks everything the composer checks: author only, trimmed, capped, audience
restricted to `everyone` / `connections`, a Circle post keeps its room's
audience, and a post with no picture cannot be left with no words. The media is
whatever you uploaded and does not change. The sheet is reached from the dots
menu and marks itself Save-disabled until something has actually changed.

**4. The post view has three dots.** Its top bar had exactly one control and it
was **Delete**, so the single destructive action in the app was also the easiest
one to hit. The dots are now a menu for every post: send to a Whisper, keep for
later, copy the words, share outside Crushly — and, on your own post, edit and
delete; on somebody else's, report or block. Delete asks once, from the menu,
instead of firing on a tap.

### The post view could not be opened on a video (`871dea5` report, fixed here)

Reported: *"the post viewer can't be accessed after implementing the pause and
play animation."* Reproduced in a browser before touching anything, by tapping
the middle of a clip in the feed and reading the navigator's state:

| Tap | Route afterwards |
|---|---|
| a **photo** post | `PostViewer { postId: 101 }` |
| a **video** post | *unchanged* — still on the feed |

**Why:** the play/pause affordance was drawn as a Pressable covering the whole
picture (`position: absolute; top/left/right/bottom: 0`). In the feed that
picture sits inside the card's own Pressable, whose job is to open the post. The
inner control claimed the touch, so every tap on a clip toggled playback and the
post itself could never be opened — and because a playing clip and a stopped clip
look the same, the tap appeared to do nothing at all.

The control was right for a player that owns the screen (the post view, where
there is nothing underneath to reach) and wrong in a feed card, where the whole
surface already means something.

**The fix** is a second shape for the same control:

- `PlayPauseFlash` gains `inline` — a 44pt disc at the bottom-right that stops its
  own taps from reaching the card underneath (`stopPropagation`), instead of a
  surface over everything.
- `EditedClip.controls` accepts `'corner'`, and the feed passes it. The post view
  keeps the full-surface disc, its flash animation and its "Paused" label.
- Nothing else in the app nests a control inside an "open" pressable: the two
  `controls` call sites were the feed and the post view, and only one of them was
  wrong.

**Verified after the fix**, same probe, same coordinates:

- tap the picture → `PostViewer { postId: 102 }` (the clip's post opens);
- tap the corner disc → stays on the feed, the clip goes from paused to playing;
- in the post view, a tap on the picture still toggles playback and shows the
  "Paused" disc.

Evidence: `evidence/flow-tap-before.png` (the tap lands, nothing opens) and
`evidence/flow-tap-after.png` (the disc in the corner, the post openable),
`evidence/viewer-paused.png` (the full-surface control, unchanged).

### The harness was lying, and that is why these two screens looked fine (`7625a9d`)

`Messages` crashed to a white page in the harness and rendered perfectly in
production — the mock, not the screen. It seeded `['conversations']` with the
*unwrapped* RPC reply (`{ items: [...] }`), string previews, `lastAt` and
`unreadCount`, none of which `list_conversations` or `ConversationSummary` ever
produce. Every field the row reads was either missing or a different type.

Three fixes, all in `devtools/`:

* **The cache seeds what the service returns**, not what the RPC returns.
* **The RPCs are answered at the transport** — `supabase.rpc` is swapped for a
  table in `mocks.ts`, in the `{ data, error }` envelope `service.rpc()`
  destructures. Screens that go through their real hooks (`useChat` calls the
  service directly, not the cache) now render for the first time.
* **The harness mounts the real `ErrorBoundary`**, so a harness crash looks like
  what a member sees rather than a white page.

`Messages` and `Chat` both draw; the fixture carries a run of three, a photo, a
day boundary and a read receipt so the grouping has something to get wrong.

### Edit Space: she/her is a real answer, and saving ends the edit (`a00edf8`)

First screen of the account thread, rendered before it was read — five panels,
`evidence/edit-0…4.png`.

**`she/her` was not a pronoun the app could express.** `PRONOUNS` in
`lib/catalog.ts` held `he/him, he/they, they/them` and nothing else, and
EditProfile decides between chip and free text by testing membership of that
list. So a member whose pronouns were `she/her` — the most common answer in the
language — saw **no chip selected** and their pronouns sitting in the
"Or write your own" box, which is the screen gently telling them their answer was
an odd one and inviting them to retype it. Onboarding reads the same list, so it
had the same hole. Now `she/her, he/him, they/them, she/they, he/they`, and a
pronoun outside the list still reads as deliberate free text.

**Hitting a cap was a dead tap.** At ten interests the eleventh tap returned the
list unchanged and said nothing — indistinguishable from a broken button. The cap
now answers ("That's 10 interests — tap one to swap it out for another"), turning
a selection *off* always works so the cap is never a trap, and Languages carries
its count the way Interests already did.

**Saving left the screen dirty.** The profile write came back first and re-seeded
the form; the @handle write landed after it. The form ended up holding the old
handle while the screen held the new one, so "Save changes" stayed in the footer
of a screen with nothing left to save, leaving asked to discard work that had
already been saved, and a second save tried to change a handle that may only
change once a day. The form is now compared against a baseline the screen owns —
seeded on load, re-seeded from the server's copy plus the handle the database
confirmed.

**Also:** the birthday printed `11/04/1998`, which is April in Lagos and November
in New York, on a line the reader cannot edit; it now says `11 April 1998`.

The form, the baseline comparison and the two formatters live in
`lib/profileForm.ts` — this logic decides whether a footer button appears and
whether leaving prompts, so it should not be reachable only through a screen.
**8 new tests; 165 pass, 0 fail; `tsc --noEmit` clean.**

Proof: `evidence/edit-pronouns-before-after.png` (a saved she/her falling into the
free-text box, then reading as a selected chip) and `evidence/edit-cap-toast.png`
(10/10 held, with the message shown).

**One correction found while doing it:** the harness was mocking

### Video: the frame follows the picture, and it can be paused (`ed782c1`)

Two complaints, both real, both structural rather than taste.

**"The video is zoomed or cropped."** Three faults stacked:

1. The feed drew *every* post in a photo's frame — `mediaRatio ?? 0.8`, a 4:5
   box — because a video had no measured ratio of its own. A 16:9 clip in a 4:5
   box must be either cropped or letterboxed; there was no third option.
2. `position:absolute; inset:0` **does not size a replaced element**. The
   `<video>` stayed at its intrinsic 1280×720 inside a 348×435 box and was
   clipped from the top-left, while `object-fit` had no box to act in and did
   nothing. Explicit `width/height: 100%` is what makes `object-fit` real.
3. A `maxHeight` cap then squashed the frame's shape instead of narrowing it.

The rule now, in one line: **the frame follows the source unless the creator
chose a shape, and cropping happens only because of that choice.**
`lib/videoFrame.ts` decides it (`videoRatio`, `aspectRatioOf`, `videoFrame`,
`clampRatio` 0.3–3, `frameHeight`), and `EditedClip` measures the clip itself —
`videoWidth/videoHeight` from a metadata `<video>` on the web,
`player.videoTrack.size` polled every 250 ms on a phone.

**Measured in the render harness, not reasoned about:** a 1280×720 clip gets a
**348×196** frame (ratio 1.778, exact 16:9); a 720×1280 clip gets **293×520**,
keeping 9:16 by narrowing to the height cap instead of being squashed.

**"No pause and play animation."** `nativeControls` is deliberately off app-wide
(grey, covers captions, unstylable), so `PlayPauseFlash` replaces it: a
tap-to-toggle disc that springs in and fades while playing, stays while paused,
respects Reduce Motion, and reads its state from `player.playing` so an auto-stop
can never leave a lying "playing" disc. Proven: tapping flips the web `<video>`'s
`!paused` to false and `currentTime` holds.

Before/after: `evidence/video-before-after.png` (three panels),
`evidence/video-portrait-frame.png`.

`tsc --noEmit` clean; **157 library tests pass** (was 150), including 7 new cases
for the frame maths — null, zero, negative and NaN sizes degrade to the fallback
rather than to an Infinity/NaN style, which is a crash on Android.
