### Edit Space: she/her is a real answer, and saving ends the edit (`a00edf8`)

First screen of the account thread, rendered before it was read — five panels,
`evidence/edit-0…4.png`.

**`she/her` was not a pronoun the app could express.** `PRONOUNS` in
`lib/catalog.ts` held `he/him, he/they, they/them` and nothing else, and
EditProfile decides between chip and free text by testing membership of that
list. So a member whose pronouns were `she/her` — the most common answer in the
language — saw **no chip selected** and their pronouns sitting in the
"Or write your own" box, which is the screen gently telling them their answer was
an odd one and inviting them to retype it. Onboarding reads the same list, so it
had the same hole. Now `she/her, he/him, they/them, she/they, he/they`, and a
pronoun outside the list still reads as deliberate free text.

**Hitting a cap was a dead tap.** At ten interests the eleventh tap returned the
list unchanged and said nothing — indistinguishable from a broken button. The cap
now answers ("That's 10 interests — tap one to swap it out for another"), turning
a selection *off* always works so the cap is never a trap, and Languages carries
its count the way Interests already did.

**Saving left the screen dirty.** The profile write came back first and re-seeded
the form; the @handle write landed after it. The form ended up holding the old
handle while the screen held the new one, so "Save changes" stayed in the footer
of a screen with nothing left to save, leaving asked to discard work that had
already been saved, and a second save tried to change a handle that may only
change once a day. The form is now compared against a baseline the screen owns —
seeded on load, re-seeded from the server's copy plus the handle the database
confirmed.

**Also:** the birthday printed `11/04/1998`, which is April in Lagos and November
in New York, on a line the reader cannot edit; it now says `11 April 1998`.

The form, the baseline comparison and the two formatters live in
`lib/profileForm.ts` — this logic decides whether a footer button appears and
whether leaving prompts, so it should not be reachable only through a screen.
**8 new tests; 165 pass, 0 fail; `tsc --noEmit` clean.**

Proof: `evidence/edit-pronouns-before-after.png` (a saved she/her falling into the
free-text box, then reading as a selected chip) and `evidence/edit-cap-toast.png`

### Found and not yet fixed: another person's Space crashes to a white screen

Opening the next screen in the thread in the harness did not render at all —
`TypeError: Cannot read properties of undefined (reading 'length')` thrown inside
`UserProfileScreen`, so the screen is a blank white page with no error state and
no way back except the OS.

`loadProfile()` (`api/service.ts:808`) hands the screen whatever `load_profile`
returns, with no normalisation, and the screen then reads `p.moments.length`,
`p.intentions.length`, `p.interests.length` and `p.sharedInterests.length` as if
they were guaranteed arrays. Any row missing one of those keys — an older profile,
a null column, a payload shape that drifts — is a crash rather than a sparse
profile.

Caught on the way past: the shared `ErrorState` prints the raw exception
(`TypeError: Failed to fetch`) as the body copy, and 14 call sites pass
`(err as Error).message` straight into it. Members should never read a stack
trace, an `apikey` complaint or a Postgres constraint name.

Both are queued next; neither is deployed.

### Video: the frame follows the picture, and it can be paused (`ed782c1`)

Two complaints, both real, both structural rather than taste.

**"The video is zoomed or cropped."** Three faults stacked:

1. The feed drew *every* post in a photo's frame — `mediaRatio ?? 0.8`, a 4:5
   box — because a video had no measured ratio of its own. A 16:9 clip in a 4:5
   box must be either cropped or letterboxed; there was no third option.
2. `position:absolute; inset:0` **does not size a replaced element**. The
   `<video>` stayed at its intrinsic 1280×720 inside a 348×435 box and was
   clipped from the top-left, while `object-fit` had no box to act in and did
   nothing. Explicit `width/height: 100%` is what makes `object-fit` real.
3. A `maxHeight` cap then squashed the frame's shape instead of narrowing it.

The rule now, in one line: **the frame follows the source unless the creator
chose a shape, and cropping happens only because of that choice.**
`lib/videoFrame.ts` decides it (`videoRatio`, `aspectRatioOf`, `videoFrame`,
`clampRatio` 0.3–3, `frameHeight`), and `EditedClip` measures the clip itself —
`videoWidth/videoHeight` from a metadata `<video>` on the web,
`player.videoTrack.size` polled every 250 ms on a phone.

**Measured in the render harness, not reasoned about:** a 1280×720 clip gets a
**348×196** frame (ratio 1.778, exact 16:9); a 720×1280 clip gets **293×520**,
keeping 9:16 by narrowing to the height cap instead of being squashed.

**"No pause and play animation."** `nativeControls` is deliberately off app-wide
(grey, covers captions, unstylable), so `PlayPauseFlash` replaces it: a
tap-to-toggle disc that springs in and fades while playing, stays while paused,
respects Reduce Motion, and reads its state from `player.playing` so an auto-stop
can never leave a lying "playing" disc. Proven: tapping flips the web `<video>`'s
`!paused` to false and `currentTime` holds.

Before/after: `evidence/video-before-after.png` (three panels),
`evidence/video-portrait-frame.png`.
